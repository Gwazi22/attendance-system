How to use this document: Each section heading below is followed by a brief italicised description of its purpose and expected content. Candidates should treat these as guidance, not rigid prescriptions; supervisors retain discretion to adapt requirements to the demands of a given study.
PRELIMINARY PAGES
These pages precede the body of the thesis and are paginated with lower-case roman numerals (i, ii, iii …). They appear in the order listed below.
Cover Page
The outer cover bearing the thesis title, the candidate's full name, the degree sought, the department, and the month and year of submission.
Title Page
Replicates the cover page with the addition of the registration number and the standard award statement ("A thesis submitted to … in partial fulfilment of the requirements for the award of …").
Certification
Signed page on which the supervisor(s) attest that the work is original to the candidate and was carried out under their supervision.
Approval
Page bearing the signatures of the internal examiner, external examiner, Head of Department, confirming that the thesis has been examined and accepted.
Dedication
A brief, one-paragraph personal statement dedicating the work to person(s) of the candidate's choosing.
Acknowledgements
Recognition of individuals, institutions and funding bodies that contributed materially or intellectually to the completion of the work.
Abstract
A self-contained summary of 150–250 words covering the problem, objectives, methodology, principal findings, and contribution to knowledge. Written last but placed here.
Table of Contents
Auto-generated listing of all chapters, sections and subsections with corresponding page numbers. Should mirror the heading hierarchy used throughout the document.
List of Tables, List of Figures, List of Plates (where applicable)
Sequential, chapter-based listings of all tables, figures and plates, each with its caption and page number. Include only those lists that apply.
Note: Plates are photographs; figures are diagrams, charts or graphs. The two are frequently and incorrectly conflated.


CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION
Establishes the research context, articulates the problem, and states what the study sets out to achieve.
1.1 Background to the Study
Contextual narrative that situates the work within its broader subject area and traces the developments that give rise to the inquiry.
1.2 Motivation of the Study
The personal, societal, scientific or industrial drivers explaining why this particular study is being undertaken at this time.
1.3 Statement of the Problem
A precise articulation of the specific gap, deficiency or unresolved question the study addresses. Should be sharp, evidence-backed and free of generalities. You must cite other others with their viewpoints concerning this problem.
1.4 Aim and Objectives of the Study
States the overarching purpose of the study and the concrete steps by which it will be achieved.
Aim: "What" is to be done — i.e. the deliverable/output of the work: a theory, software framework, model, algorithm, system, etc.
Objectives: "How" the aim is to be achieved. Objectives must be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) and each should begin with an action verb.
1.5 Significance of the Study
The expected value of the work to academia, industry, government and end-users. Answers the "so what?" question.
1.6 Scope of the Study
The boundaries of the work — what is covered (domain, dataset, period, geography, target users) and what is intentionally excluded.
1.7 Limitations of the Study
Constraints encountered (data access, computational resources, time, sample size) that may bound the generalisability of the findings.
1.8 Operational Definition of Terms
Definitions of key technical terms as they are used in this specific work, distinguishing the operational sense from the ordinary dictionary meaning.


CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW
A systematic, critical synthesis of prior scholarship that locates the present study within the existing body of knowledge.
Approach: Adopt a systematic literature review conforming to the 5Cs — Cite, Compare, Contrast, Critique, Connect.
2.1 Conceptual/Theoretical Review
Survey of the foundational theories, models and paradigms that underpin the study. Subsections may be tailored to suit the specific work.
2.2 Review of Related Works
Critical examination of empirical works by other authors that are directly relevant to the project. A minimum of 15 sources, with at least 60% published in the last five years, is expected where applicable.
2.3 Summary of Literature Review
A condensed synthesis of the reviewed works, presented in a meta-analysis table that exposes patterns, strengths, weaknesses and unresolved issues.

2.4 Research Gap
A focused statement of the specific deficiencies the present study addresses, drawn directly from the synthesis in Section 2.3 and used to justify the work.


CHAPTER THREE: METHODOLOGY
Sets out the theories, techniques and tools used to realise the aim and objectives. This is the chapter against which methodological rigour is judged.
Note: All figures, tables and equations must be discussed in the body of the text; none should appear without commentary. Number them on a chapter basis (e.g. Figure 3.2, Table 3.1).
3.1 Theoretical Framework
The underpinning theories that justify the chosen approach. A postgraduate work without an explicit theoretical anchor will not be accepted — a PG thesis is not a project.
3.2 Conceptual Framework
Visual and narrative representation of the proposed solution, including system architecture, component diagrams, and process flows in both as-is and to-be forms.

3.2.1Development Methodology (SDLC)
State the model you selected (Agile/Scrum, Iterative Waterfall, V-Model) and why for your project size. Include Figure 3.1: Adopted SDLC Model then discuss it in text.

3.2.2Requirement Engineering
Requirement gathering techniques: Describe which you used, sample size, and why. 
Functional Requirements:  list FR1, FR2... with description. Discuss each group in text.
 Non-Functional Requirements: performance, security, usability, etc.

3.2.3System Design (UML)
Have at least 4 UML diagrams (Use Case Diagram, Class Diagram, Sequence/Activity Diagram etc.), every diagram gets a paragraph explaining the diagram

3.2.4Architectural Design
Present your architecture (3-tier, MVC, client-server, microservices). discuss layers and communication of system architecture.

3.2.5Database Design
Outline system structural blueprint. It requires a visual Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) to map out data relationships, a detailed database schema defining the tables, fields, data types, and keys, and a concluding discussion on the normalization level (such as 3NF) to ensure data integrity and reduce redundancy.

3.3 Mathematical Framework – optional for BSc (only include if you have formulas)
Formal definitions, equations, derivations and (where relevant) proofs that underlie the proposed model. Equations must be typeset using the equation editor, not as images or plain text.
3.4 Computational Framework
Algorithms, pseudocode, complexity analysis and the computational logic that operationalise the mathematical framework into something executable.
3.5 Tools
Hardware specifications, software stack, programming languages, libraries, frameworks, datasets and evaluation platforms used to implement and validate the work.

CHAPTER FOUR: RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Presents the outputs produced by applying the methodology of Chapter Three and interprets them against the objectives stated in Section 1.4.
Note: The depth of results is a function of the depth of the design; the depth of discussion is a function of the depth of the results. Use infographics to visualise tabular data where appropriate and must include screenshots of the live simulation/development environment.


CHAPTER FIVE: SUMMARY, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Closes the thesis by recapping the work, stating what has been established, and pointing forward to applications and future research.
5.1 Summary
A brief, non-evaluative recap of the problem, methods and principal findings. No new material should be introduced here.
5.2 Conclusion
Definitive statements of what the study has established, addressing each objective in turn and grounded strictly in the results.
5.3 Recommendations
Actionable suggestions for academics, practitioners, regulators and policy makers, arising directly from the findings.
5.3.1 Application Areas
Specific domains, sectors and use-cases where the deliverable can be deployed for practical benefit.
5.3.2 Suggestions for Further Research
Open questions, methodological refinements and adjacent problems that the present study has surfaced but not resolved.
5.4 Contributions to Knowledge
A numbered list of the novel contributions made by the thesis. Each contribution should map back to an objective in Section 1.4.
REFERENCES
A complete alphabetical listing of every source cited in the body of the thesis, formatted strictly in APA 7th edition style. Uncited sources should not appear in this list.
APPENDICES
Supplementary material that supports but does not interrupt the main narrative. Each appendix should be referenced at least once in the body of the thesis.
Appendix A: Sample of Source Code
Annotated extracts of the source code central to the implementation. Full repositories may be referenced by URL or DOI.
Appendix B: Sample of Outputs
Screenshots, console logs and result artefacts that supplement those discussed in Chapter Four.
Appendix C: Questionnaire (where applicable)
A clean copy of the instrument used for primary data collection — questionnaire, interview schedule, or observation guide.


GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
✓Language: Write in clear, formal British English. Use third-person passive where appropriate for methodology (“The system was designed...”). Avoid slang and pidgin.
✓Originality: The project must be your own work. All code, diagrams, and text taken from others must be cited.
✓Plagiarism: Maximum similarity index of <20% on plagiarism/AI detector (excluding references and code listings).
✓Ethics: Projects involving human data, questionnaires, or personal information require departmental ethical clearance.
✓Supervision: Minimum of six documented supervisor meetings. Logbook must be signed.
